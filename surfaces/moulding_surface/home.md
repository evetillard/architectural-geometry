## Definition and form generation

A moulding surface is generated by sweeping a fixed planar profile along a planar directrix. The profile preserves its shape and moves without twisting, with its plane remaining perpendicular to the tangent of the directrix. The profile (the generatrix) and the directrix do not play interchangeable roles. A moulding surface may be represented parametrically as [@ferreol2020moulding]: 
$$
\mathbf{S}(u,v) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}(u) + \boldsymbol{g}(v) \vec{\boldsymbol{n}}(u) + \boldsymbol{h}(v) \vec{\boldsymbol{b}}
$$

- $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(u)$ : <span style="color: rgb(255, 158, 0);">directrix</span>
- $\boldsymbol{g}(v), \boldsymbol{h}(v)$ : coordinate functions defining the <span style="color: rgb(0, 112, 192);">profile curve/generatrix</span>
- $\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}(u)$ : unit normal vector to the directrix in its plane
- $\vec{\boldsymbol{b}}$ : constant unit vector normal to the plane of the directrix

The construction may be understood as the profile curve occupying a continuous sequence of positions along the directrix, without changing its shape or size. At each position, its plane is reoriented to remain perpendicular to the tangent of the directrix.
This operation differs from a curve extrusion. In an extrusion, the profile retains a fixed orientation in space as it follows the path. It therefore ceases, in general, to be perpendicular to the path tangent. In a moulding sweep, the profile is instead reoriented continuously so that its plane remains normal to the directrix. The two operations coincide when the directrix is a straight line and the profile lies in a plane perpendicular to it.
