## Definition and properties

The Gaussian curvature $K$ at a point on a surface is the product of the two principal curvatures.

$$
K = k_1 \cdot k_2
$$
Its sign indicates how the surface bends locally.

### Positive Gaussian curvature $K>0$ 

The two principal curvatures have the same sign. The surface bends in the same direction along both principal directions, and the point is called **elliptic**. When this occurs throughout the surface, the surface is described as **synclastic**.

*Examples : Sphere, Ellipsoid*

### Zero Gaussian curvature $K=0$

At least one of the two principal curvatures is zero. The surface has no double curvature at that point, which is called **parabolic**. When this condition holds throughout a regular surface, the surface is singly curved if exactly one principal curvature is zero, and planar if both principal curvatures are zero.

*Examples : Cylinder*

### Negative Gaussian curvature $K<0$

The two principal curvatures have opposite signs. The surface bends in opposite directions along its two principal directions, and the point is called **hyperbolic**. When this occurs throughout the surface, the surface is described as **anticlastic**.

*Examples : [Hyperbolic Paraboloid](../../surfaces/hyperbolic_paraboloid/home.md), Hyperboloid*
